In mutualised logistics networks the urgency an operator declares often diverges from the
urgency computable from the operational state. Over-declaration wastes shared capacity;
under-declaration hides rupture risk. No existing supply chain digital twin measures that
divergence or propagates it across a supplier network.

This thesis builds and tests one. Real urgency is aggregated at each node from six KPI
blocks through a weighted probabilistic OR and propagated upstream across a directed acyclic
supply graph; declared urgency is elicited through a consistency-gated pairwise questionnaire
and propagated downstream; their mismatch is scored on $[0,100]$ with an asymmetric
Prospect-Theory penalty. It was evaluated through two pre-registered campaigns with
language-model declarants: a nineteen-turn replay of the 2020 to 2022 semiconductor shortage
over eight nodes, with a blind control arm and a forecast-assisted arm on one frozen scenario;
and an airframer supply base of fifteen nodes carrying no exogenous shock, whose milestone
truth is derived from the indicator trajectory rather than scripted.

Three results follow. As a device for deciding which supplier to attend to, the gap works and
works early: over the six turns preceding the public onset of the crisis it flags only nodes
that later miss a delivery commitment, three of the four that eventually do, and the worst is
flagged eight turns before its miss while that node's own manager declares an urgency of
$0.001$. The second chain bounds that claim: where declarants judge their state correctly and
simply withhold escalation, the gap selects nothing, so the instrument detects misperception
and not discretion. As a device for attaching a calibrated
probability to a named week it fails, at a Brier skill between $-3.4$ and $-6.9$ against a
base-rate forecaster, with twenty-one confident predictions of which none materialised. One
block explains it: the time block reads a lead-time distribution that no shock ever reached.
The repair is not the obvious one, since lost time must be added to the completion date
rather than multiplied into it, and it halves the Brier deficit without making it positive.

Two methodological findings generalise beyond this system. The definition of an adverse
outcome moves the reported calibration by a factor of five while leaving discrimination
unchanged. And displaying a forecast to the declarant transfers authority from the shop floor
to the model, in whichever direction the model points, on a layer that a two-number calendar
ratio outranks at six horizons of eight. Every rate above is bounded by the sample rather than
the construction: two scenarios, twenty-three nodes, seven positives. The instrument and its
diagnostic method transfer; the rates do not yet.
