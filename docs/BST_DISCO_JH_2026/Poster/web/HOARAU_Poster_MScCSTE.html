Three sites, three stages: roof segmented, shape and area read from it, then the docks

WHAT SEGMENTING THE ROOF FIRST CHANGED Roof area lands within 8 %, 1 % and 2 % of the reference polygon, so measuring a footprint from imagery alone holds up. Layout is read off the roof's medial axis rather than off corner counts, restricted to the three classes a warehouse actually takes: I, L, U. The reference database returned "complex" for all three of these sites because it splits one warehouse across several records; the segmented roof reads I, I and L instead. On the third site the new order also removed 11 docks that the footprint-first run had credited to it. They belong to the neighbour across the courtyard and fall outside this roof.

S3 The 2020 to 2022 chip shortage replayed via a Serious Game 8 nodes, 18 turns

Hélios reimagines the chip shortage as a serious game and proof of concept, demonstrating how shortages can be anticipated with sufficient data. Beyond replaying past crises, it can support scenario simulation and decision-making by identifying the most likely outcomes. Using documented disruptions from September 2020 to February 2022 and urgency scores derived from frozen public datasets, the model naturally reveals the foundry as the key bottleneck without being explicitly instructed to do so.

WHAT THE CAMPAIGN ACTUALLY SHOWED - The twin identified the real bottleneck on its own: the foundry ranked first in 7/10 informative turns using only physical KPIs, matching the industry's actual 2021 constraint. Even without injected events, urgency rose across the network (0.34 to 0.47) on weak-signal turns.
- Contagion effects were also observed. Nodes without physical disruptions increased their declared urgency on press-driven turns, with some actors reacting several turns before local impacts appeared, while downstream players lagged behind.
- The hidden-risk detector missed events at the predefined threshold, but the underlying signal remained visible: bad-outcome rates increased from 10% to 25% as hidden-risk scores rose, suggesting the threshold should be lowered.
S4 · WHAT IS LEFT TO STUDY Run the campaign with the eight human consultants over the densest window of the crisis, which the protocol sizes at 72 observations. Refit the hidden-risk threshold and the asymmetry on that data rather than on literature defaults. Use other AI to generate the series that have no public source, so the model can move from a weekly review toward a real-time decision aid driven by urgency. Then compare the turn the model saw criticality rise against the date the trade press reported the same rise, on anchors such as Broadcom lead times moving from 12.2 to 22.2 weeks.

V + S What this gives a real supply-chain simulation

Measured, not asked

Storable volume, pallet slots, dock count, roof height, building shape, loading apron length and yard depth. For every warehouse in France, without a phone call or a site visit, and repeatable on any date or location where the imagery exists.

The nodes are the missing layer

In a supply-chain twin the arcs are public: roads, distances, costs, emissions. The nodes are not. Capacity is private, declared, or absent. Nobody publishes that a given building holds 34 000 pallets and has 42 docks. Imagery produces that layer: every building above 1 000 m², the same constants everywhere, nobody's consent needed. Without it a network model runs on synthetic nodes, the classic weakness of Physical Internet simulations.

Stock against flow, and the investment signal

A node is defined by throughput against stock, not by volume. A transit hub has many docks and little volume; an inventory point is the reverse. Occupancy is the only outside measure of dormant capacity, which is the whole promise of pooling. Imagery is re-acquired every year, so re-running turns a snapshot into a time series. That is the investment signal, and every constant ships with its uncertainty.
