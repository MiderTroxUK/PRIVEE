\paragraph{Quantitatively.}
The delivered system covers the specified scope and is measurably robust: 1\,831 automated
test functions expanding to 2\,044 collected cases, an auditable write path, and performance
validated at the intended scale. The experiment produced numbers rather than impressions: an
area under the ROC curve between $0.37$ and $0.56$, a Brier skill between $-3.4$ and $-6.9$,
twenty-one confident forecasts of which none materialised against the production definition
of an adverse outcome, and an eight-of-eight one-sided response at first exposure to the
displayed forecast.

The repair that followed is measured on the same frozen forecasts. The Brier skill improves
from $-1.278$ to $-0.687$ at one week and from $-0.553$ to $-0.216$ at four, roughly halving
the calibration deficit at every horizon, while the structural half of the repair also raises
the area under the ROC curve at every horizon. Three figures qualify that. The skill is still
negative, so the forecast remains worse calibrated than announcing the base rate. The
milestone probability is still binary in seventy-two percent of cases, against eighty-five
before. And the closed-loop campaign run on the repaired system met five of eleven milestones
on their original date, exactly as many as the frozen run: it changed which milestone was
met, not how many.

\paragraph{Qualitatively.}
The mission set out to determine whether the declared-versus-real gap can be measured well
enough to act on. On the evidence gathered it can, at the level a coordinator actually works
at, which is deciding where to look. The gap flags the right suppliers, it flags them before
the crisis is visible to anyone, and it does so on nodes whose own managers are declaring
that all is well. What is not yet established is the next step up, turning that flag into a
calibrated probability attached to a named week.

The work is worth more to the programme for having failed that second step first. The failure
was actionable: it named one component, stated the mechanism, and specified the repair, and
the repair was then carried out and measured. A favourable score obtained on the first pass,
on a signal driven by a block that could not see the crisis, would have been worse than
useless, because it would have been trusted. The route from here is not a new idea but more
data, and Section~\ref{sec:perspectives} says which data and why.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion_limites}

Five limits bound what this report supports, and four of the five are limits of sample
rather than of design. That distinction matters for what happens next: none of them calls the
construction into question, and all of them are answered by running the instrument on more
chains rather than by changing it.

The declarants were agents rather than human consultants, so every statement about
declaration behaviour applies to that population. Results come from a single scenario, so the
outcome base rate and event mix are properties of one script. The positive class is very
small, eight nodes of which four are positive and eleven observable milestones, which bounds
every claim in both directions: it is why the precision of $1.00$ in
Section~\ref{sec:ciblage} is reported as an encouraging observation rather than a rate, and
why the movements in the area under the ROC curve in Section~\ref{sec:reparation} are stated
as uninterpretable rather than as gains. And the carbon block was the least exercised of the
six, for want of data to feed it, which is the measurement gap noted in
Section~\ref{sec:rse}; the indicator completion of Section~\ref{sec:reparation} narrowed it
without closing it.

The fifth is methodological and does not dissolve with more data. Every score depends on
which of the three outcome definitions of Section~\ref{sec:cible} it is read against, and no
number in this report should be quoted without one.

\subsection{Perspectives}
\label{sec:perspectives}

\paragraph{For ALTEN.}
The binding constraint from here is data, not design. The measurement works at node level on
the chain it was tested on; what stands between that and a deployable early-warning
instrument is the number of chains, nodes and milestones it has been run against, and the
steps below are ordered accordingly.

The first of the four steps this report recommended has been taken, and taking it changed the
recommendation. Repairing the time block by making the calibrated events write a lead-time
impact, as originally proposed, does not work: routing lost time into the lead time makes it
scale with the work remaining, so the shock vanishes on exactly the milestones nearest their
deadline. Lost time has to be added to the completion date, not multiplied into it
(Section~\ref{sec:reparation}). The step generalised into a rule worth keeping beyond this
system: distinguish a parameter describing a node's recovery capability from a state
accumulating what it has actually lost, because the two live naturally in the same units and
mixing them is invisible until the data is complete.

Four steps remain, in order. Fill the indicators before calibrating anything, since the
campaign that produced the negative result ran on a quarter of them and half the urgency
blocks were inert; no amount of calibration compensates for a missing input, and one real bug
was undetectable until the database was full. Rebuild the post-hoc calibration layer's
training label on the target the product actually displays, which first requires the
synthetic factory's snapshots to carry milestone truth; the layer as trained today improves
calibration and degrades ranking, and is deliberately not connected. Make the forecast state
its own ignorance, since a layer that cannot anticipate exogenous shocks should not present a
low probability in a confident numeric format, and smooth the milestone alarm, which today
moves from $0.05$ to $1.00$ in a single turn and is correct but useless as advance warning.
Then run the campaign with human participants, which is the only run that can decide the
hypotheses as they were written.

One indicator class is missing rather than empty, and several declarants named it
independently: the forward booking horizon, how far ahead a node's capacity is already sold.
In the real shortage that signal preceded the degradation of every flow indicator by months,
and it is structurally invisible to the model as built. It is a general gap, not a
semiconductor one: any capacity-constrained link holds that information.

Beyond that, the adequacy layer is designed to be coupled to DISCO rather than to remain
standalone, and the network representation work carried out in parallel on the same programme
is the natural counterpart to connect it to.

\paragraph{For the author.}
The subject continues in the research thesis produced for Cranfield University, which treats
the same measurements at greater depth. The open question I would most like to pursue is the
one the treatment arm opened: what an advisory layer owes the person reading it when it does
not know.

\subsection{Personal Assessment}
\label{sec:bilan}

\subsubsection{What I learned}

The hardest problem of these six months was not mathematical. It was recognising, three
separate times and in three different disguises, that the system was measuring itself. Each
time the symptom looked like a modelling defect and each time the cause was circularity in
the measurement. I would now design the independence of two signals before designing either
of them.

The second lesson concerns what counts as a result. For a long stretch I treated a negative
outcome as a failure to be fixed before reporting it. The most useful findings in this report
are negative, and none would have been found by a project optimising for a positive one. The
related discipline, which the rejected dataset taught quickly, is that a confident
presentation is not evidence, and that the cheap verification step belongs before the
expensive one.

With hindsight I would change two things. I would have scored the forecast layer against
recorded outcomes far earlier, since doing so took a few hundred lines and revealed the
defect immediately where months of implementation had not. And I would have written the
outcome definition into the pre-registration alongside the thresholds, having since measured
how much it moves the answer.

\subsubsection{What this mission clarified professionally}

I came into this placement expecting the interesting difficulty to be in the mathematics. It
was not. The mathematics behaved; what failed was the join between a model and the world it
was supposed to describe, and then the join between a model's output and the person meant to
act on it. Both failures were only visible because the work was measured against something
external rather than judged on its own internal consistency.

That is the kind of engineering I want to continue: the boundary where a model meets the
person who has to act on it. It is where the failures are least obvious and most consequential,
and this mission was a fairly direct demonstration of why.
