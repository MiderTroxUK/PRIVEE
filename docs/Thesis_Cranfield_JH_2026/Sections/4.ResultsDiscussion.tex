\subsection{Introduction}
\label{sec:res_intro}
%% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

This section reports what was measured, in six parts, and the order matters because the
answer is not uniform across them. The delivered system and its mechanical validation come
first, establishing that the pipeline runs end to end. The blind declaration arm follows,
giving the pre-registered hypothesis verdicts. The adequacy signal is then scored on the
decision it was built to support, choosing which nodes to attend to, where it performs well
and early. The forecast layer built on top of that signal is scored next against recorded
outcomes, which is where this thesis found its defect and, through it, a diagnosis of the
mechanism behind it; the repair that followed is measured on the same frozen forecasts. The
forecast-assisted arm measures what happens when a forecast is placed in front of the
declarant. Finally, a short opening reports a separate system that addresses the freshness
limitation the rest of this work documents about itself.

Two claims are worth separating in advance, because conflating them is the easiest way to
misread what follows. That the gap between declared and computed urgency carries information
about which supplier will fail is supported by the campaign. That the gap can be converted
into a well-calibrated probability attached to a named week is not, yet. The distance between
those two statements is the subject of Sections~\ref{sec:res_armA_scored}
and~\ref{sec:res_repair}, and the sample that bounds both is stated wherever a figure is
given.

Every number below is regenerated from raw artifacts by the derivation script described in
Section~\ref{sec:methods_repro}.

\subsection{The Delivered System}
\label{sec:res_system}
%% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

SupplyScore is delivered as a standalone Python application: 73 modules across 10 packages,
covered by 1\,831 automated test functions in 106 test files, driving a multi-page Dash web
interface over a multi-tenant SQLite persistence layer. The suite expands to 2\,044 collected
cases, of which 2\,028 pass and 15 are skipped for want of an optional dependency; one fails
on two missing image files referenced by a presentation script outside the library. All of the mathematical objects of
Section~\ref{sec:math_framework} are implemented and exercised: the six-block noisy-OR
aggregation, the AHP elicitation with its consistency gate, the Fuzzy BWM weighting, the
bidirectional DAG propagation, the asymmetric adequacy score, the Monte Carlo lead-time
estimate, the PROMETHEE~II network outranking and the systematic shock-simulation
criticality index.

Three operational capabilities matter for what follows rather than being peripheral. The
per-node explainability page reconstructs any score term by term, which is what allowed the
diagnosis in Section~\ref{sec:res_mechanism} to be made from the application rather than by
hand. The Excel export produces frozen data for analysis outside the tool, which the
campaign relies on. The hardened persistence layer, with write-ahead logging, pre-backup
integrity checks and automatic snapshots, is what makes a campaign database an auditable
record weeks after the run.

Two pages carry most of the operator's interaction with the system.
Figure~\ref{fig:hebdo} shows the weekly review, whose four sections commit as one signed
transaction so that no partial review can enter a node's history.
Figure~\ref{fig:criticite} shows the ranking the shock simulation produces, which is the

\subsection{Mechanical Validation: The Synthetic Dry Run}
\label{sec:res_dryrun}
%% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Before any declaration data existed, the full campaign mechanics were exercised end to end on
a disposable database. Five iterations calibrated the scenario and eliminated engine
artefacts, and the final iteration replayed all nineteen turns with synthetic respondents
standing in for the eight declarants.

This is an engineering check, not a scientific one. The reason is in how the synthetic
declarations were made: each one is anchored on the node's own local real urgency, following
$1 + 8 \times U_{r,\text{local}}$ with a fixed panic, under-report or neutral bias per role.
A declaration built that way cannot lag the signal it is derived from. It cannot under-report
that signal for long, and it cannot react at all to a press event it never reads. Run against
the pre-registered tests, it therefore returns not confirmed or not applicable on all six
hypotheses by construction. That is the expected outcome, and an informative one.

What it does establish is that injection, weekly decay, milestone drift, event calibration,
propagation, criticality, snapshotting and backup all run correctly under the two-turns-per-day
cadence the campaign requires. It also calibrated the null baseline detector, a raw threshold
$U_{r,\text{local}} \ge 0.5$, at precision $0.16$ and recall $0.27$: the bar the adequacy
signal must clear to demonstrate value beyond a raw KPI reading.

Figure~\ref{fig:helios_dryrun_trajectories} shows the circularity visually. Real urgency is
drawn solid and synthetic declared urgency dashed, for all eight nodes across the full
replay. The two track each other closely at every node rather than one lagging or decoupling
from the other, which is the signature of the construction and not a property the campaign is
expected to reproduce. It is also why H1, H2, H5 and H6 cannot be read off this run: each of
them asks about a divergence that the generator cannot produce.

\subsection{Arm A: Blind Declaration by Eight Isolated Agents}
\label{sec:res_armA}
%% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Arm A replaced the synthetic generator with eight isolated language-model agents, one per
role. Each was confined to its own role card and turn briefings, with no sight of the other
roles, the dashboard, or any future turn. Each read its briefing in character, then answered
the real AHP questionnaire of Section~\ref{sec:ahp_tool}; pairwise comparisons and severity
scores passed through the production consistency-ratio code untouched.

The difference from the dry run matters. This arm drove the full production pipeline.
Campaign setup, turn injection and questionnaire submission all went through the same
weekly-cycle service a browser-based participant would reach.

\subsection{What the Adequacy Signal Gets Right: Node-Level Targeting}
\label{sec:res_targeting}
%% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The scoring that follows in Section~\ref{sec:res_armA_scored} asks whether the forecast layer
attaches correct probabilities to node-weeks. That is the right question for a forecast and
the wrong question for the instrument. A coordinator does not act on the proposition that
node $X$ has a $0.14$ chance of an adverse outcome between weeks $t+1$ and $t+4$. They act on
a shortlist: which of these suppliers do I look at this week. This subsection scores that,
because it is the decision the adequacy signal was designed to support, and because the
answer differs from the one below.

\subsubsection{Protocol}

At each turn the eight nodes are ranked by hidden risk $H = [U_r - U_d]_+$, and the set
flagged at $H > 0.10$ is compared with the set of nodes that go on to miss at least one
milestone at its originally committed deadline, the definition of
Section~\ref{sec:res_target_trap}. Four of the eight nodes are positive under that definition:
the satellite prime, the avionics integrator, the electronics manufacturer and the foundry.
The comparison is at node level and prospective, since the flag at turn $t$ is confronted
with misses occurring later in the campaign. Nothing in it uses the forecast layer.

For six consecutive turns the signal flags exactly three nodes, all three of which later miss
a commitment, with no false alarm on any of the six. The fourth positive node is never
flagged early, so recall stops at three of four. On every one of those turns the worst-scoring
node of the eight is the satellite prime, which misses its turn-8 delivery and delivers at
turn 14: the flag precedes the miss by eight turns, and it is raised while that node's own
manager is declaring an urgency of $0.001$.

That last clause is the construction working as specified rather than a coincidence. The
flag does not come from a pessimistic declaration; it comes from an optimistic declaration
standing against a computed state that is not. A system aggregating indicators alone would
have ranked that node on its indicators. A system collecting declarations alone would have
believed it. Only the difference between the two carries this information, which is the
proposition the thesis set out to test and the one the literature review of
Section~\ref{sec:lit_gaps} found unaddressed.

\subsubsection{Why the Signal Degrades, and Why That Is Not a Defect}

From turn 6 the precision falls to $0.50$ and the recall to $0.25$. The mechanism is
structural. Once the shortage is public every declarant raises their urgency, $U_d$ converges
towards $U_r$, and a quantity defined as $[U_r - U_d]_+$ shrinks by construction. A
perception-gap measure cannot survive the disappearance of the perception gap, and should not
be expected to.

The consequence is a scope statement rather than a limitation to be repaired. The instrument
is an early-warning device and not a crisis-management one; its useful window is the interval
in which a discrepancy between what is declared and what is computed still exists, which is
also the interval in which acting is still cheap. Read that way, the campaign shows the
signal at its sharpest precisely where its value is highest.

\subsubsection{What the Repair Changes Here}

The repair of Section~\ref{sec:res_repair} sharpens this flag without altering which nodes it
selects. At turn 3 the satellite prime scores $26.6$ out of $100$ on the campaign as run,
against $46.0$ for the next worst node; after repair the same turn gives $2.2$ against
$44.6$. The ordering is identical, and the separation widens by an order of magnitude. The
difference is one of legibility rather than of correctness: a node at $26.6$ in a field whose
next value is $46.0$ is an anomaly a careful analyst notices, while a node at $2.2$ in a
field whose next value is $44.6$ is one no dashboard can hide.

\subsubsection{Statistical Standing}

This result rests on eight nodes, four of them positive, over one scripted scenario. A
precision of $1.00$ sustained over six turns on three flagged nodes is an encouraging
observation and not an estimated rate; with four positives, no confidence interval worth
printing can be attached to it. It is reported here because it is the outcome the instrument
was built to produce and because it points the same way as the mechanism, not because the
sample settles anything. Section~\ref{sec:future_work} states what would settle it, and the
answer is more chains rather than a different model.

\subsubsection{The Second Chain, and the Boundary It Draws}
\label{sec:res_second_chain}

A second chain was then built and run: AIRB, an airframer's supply base of fifteen nodes and
thirty arcs over five ranks, with no exogenous shock at any point and three structurally
central suppliers set to degrade endogenously. Its milestone truth is derived from the
indicator trajectory rather than scripted, so a supplier that slows down misses its milestone
because it slowed down. Fifteen declarants, one per node, each seeing only its own monthly
sheet and its own past declarations, played all nineteen turns.

On that chain the adequacy signal does not select the failing nodes at all. Across all
nineteen turns, precision at rank three is $0.00$ for both $[U_r - U_d]_+$ and the adequacy
score, against a base rate of $0.20$. The forecast layer, scored on the same runs, holds:
precision at rank three of $0.56$.

The mechanism is legible in the snapshots and is not a failure of aggregation. The three
degrading suppliers declare the highest urgencies in the chain from turn~0 onwards, before
their indicators have moved: $U_d = 0.94$, $0.88$ and $0.98$ at turn~0, still $0.97$ and
$0.96$ at turn~16 against a computed $U_r$ of $1.00$. A quantity defined as $[U_r - U_d]_+$
is then bounded by $0.03$. Nothing is concealed, so nothing is detected. The nodes that top
the gap ranking at every turn are the downstream assemblers, whose local urgency is
\emph{zero} and whose computed $U_r$ is inherited entirely through the graph from suppliers
they cannot observe.

This is a boundary rather than a refutation, and it is sharper than the scope statement above.
The role cards of those three suppliers described concealers, and the declarants behaved as
such in prose, turn after turn, writing that they would put off calling their customers and
keep overtime in reserve. At the same turns they marked near-maximal severity on the
questionnaire. They minimised in \emph{action} and maximised in \emph{measurement}. The
questionnaire asks what an actor perceives; withholding is something an actor does. A gap
defined between a computed state and a declared perception therefore detects blindness, not
discretion. The synthetic declarants of the semiconductor campaign carried a bias applied to
the number itself, so the gap opened and precision reached $1.00$; a declarant that reasons
does not lower the number.

The claim that survives both chains is therefore narrower than the one turn~14 supports on
its own: the perception gap is informative where declarants misjudge their own state, and
silent where they judge it correctly and simply do not escalate. Distinguishing those two
failure modes requires an instrument that observes escalation behaviour and not only declared
severity, which the present design does not provide. This is stated in
Section~\ref{sec:future_work} as the first item rather than as a caveat, because it bears on
what the instrument measures and not merely on how well.

\subsection{Arm A Rescored: Discrimination Without Calibration}
\label{sec:res_armA_scored}
%% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The hypothesis verdicts above test the adequacy signal at a single threshold. The forecast
layer that the system also emits, a probability of an adverse outcome at horizons of one to
four weeks, deserves a fuller treatment, because a single operating point cannot distinguish
a forecast that orders outcomes badly from one that orders them well but announces the wrong
numbers. This subsection scores all 120 prospective forecasts of arm A, covering turns 4 to
18 across eight nodes, against the production outcome definition of
Section~\ref{sec:methods_scoring}.

\subsubsection{Ruling Out Right-Censoring}

An obvious objection is that forecasts made near the end of the campaign have observation
windows extending past the last turn, so their outcomes could not be recorded even if they
occurred. If the confident forecasts were concentrated at the end, censoring rather than
miscalibration would explain the result.

They are not. The twenty-one confident forecasts are spread over turns 5 to 18 and across
seven of the eight nodes. Repeating the scoring with every forecast whose window would run
past the final turn removed leaves $n = 88$ forecasts, still 0 true positives against 16
false positives, and the Brier skill becomes worse rather than better, moving from $-3.36$ to
$-7.82$. Censoring is not the explanation.

\subsubsection{The Mechanism}
\label{sec:res_mechanism}

The confident tail has a single source, and the explainability layer of
Section~\ref{sec:explain_export} makes it visible without instrumentation. Across all
twenty-one confident forecasts, the four-week probability and the milestone-miss probability
never differ by more than $0.020$. The confident tail is the milestone channel, and nothing
else contributes to it.

Set against the registry, this is decisive. Of the eighteen milestones in the campaign, ten
completed and eight remained active, and none was abandoned. Exactly one was still open past
a deadline that had fallen inside the campaign, the Orbitalys delivery of
Table~\ref{tab:armA_snapshot}. The model announced near-certain misses for AvioSys, CompoDis,
\'Electis, NovaFab, TransGlobal and Meridian, and all of them delivered.

That registry reading is more forgiving than it first appears, and the qualification matters
enough to state here rather than in a footnote. The registry stores each milestone's
\emph{current} deadline, and a replanning overwrites it, so a milestone that is re-dated and
subsequently delivered is recorded as completed. Read instead against the deadline each node
originally committed to, six of the eleven milestones whose original deadline fell inside the
campaign were missed: five were delivered one to six turns late, and one was never delivered
at all. Under that stricter reading, five of the twenty-one confident forecasts were followed
by a missed original commitment within four weeks rather than none of them. The confident
tail remains wrong far more often than right and the mechanism below is unaffected, but the
distance between zero of twenty-one and five of twenty-one is produced entirely by which
deadline the truth is read against, which is the subject of
Section~\ref{sec:res_target_trap}.

\bottomrule

Six of the eight nodes read as numerically zero in the middle of a crisis, and the seventh
reads as saturated. The block behaves as a step function rather than as a risk gradient: it
is either off or fully on, with almost nothing in between. The cause is structural. The
block compares a lead-time distribution against the time remaining to the next deadline,
and the scripted events act on capacity, cost and failure probability without acting on any
quantity on the time axis, so a shock never moves the quantity that the milestone probability
is computed from. The strongest form of the evidence is a count that depends on no scoring
choice at all: across the whole campaign, eight nodes and 41 indicator snapshots each, the
accumulated-delay field the block would need was written a non-zero value exactly zero times.
The block is blind to the crisis by construction, and because that block drives the entire
confident tail, so is the forecast.

This is a defect in the model rather than a property of the scenario, and it is the single
most actionable finding of the campaign. It also invalidates, for this run, the assumption
recorded in Section~\ref{sec:model_blocks} that each block contributes useful information to
the aggregate. One block did not.

\subsection{The Target-Definition Trap}
\label{sec:res_target_trap}
%% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The scores above depend on a choice that is easy to overlook: what counts as an adverse
outcome. Section~\ref{sec:methods_scoring} uses the production definition, requiring a missed
milestone, an abandoned node, or an event of gravity critical or defect. Two alternatives are
worth scoring the identical forecasts against. The first is the one an evaluator without
access to the model's own definition would most likely reach for: any event recorded on the
node. The second keeps the production definition of an adverse outcome intact but reads a
missed milestone against the deadline the node originally committed to rather than against
whatever deadline it currently carries, and drops the forecasts whose observation window runs
past the last campaign turn.

The practical consequence is that a calibration figure reported without its outcome
definition is close to meaningless. This has a direct bearing on how the framework of this
thesis should be evaluated in future work: the outcome definition belongs in the
pre-registration alongside the thresholds, and it is not currently part of the H1 to H6

\subsection{Arm B: What Happens When the Forecast Is Shown}
\label{sec:res_armB}

thing: from turn 4 onward, each agent is shown the predictive analysis of its own node
before declaring its urgency. The loop stays open, so the terrain does not change and the
forecasts remain scorable.

One property of the design must be established before any interpretation. The prediction
layer is deterministic, so the two arms should carry identical forecasts on the turns they
share, and they do: all 72 shared node-turn forecasts are numerically identical between the

not from a different forecast being displayed.

before the event it had failed to anticipate.

the free-text notes shows that agents continued to take protective actions at the same turn,
including negotiating allocation, alerting the customer and ordering ahead of schedule. The
measured effect is on what was declared, not on what was done. Given that the declared value
is precisely the input the adequacy layer consumes, this is a consequential effect on the
measurement system even where it is not, on this evidence, an effect on behaviour.

\subsection{The Repair and Its Re-measurement}
\label{sec:res_repair}
%% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The campaign identified one defect and specified one repair. The repair was then carried out
and re-measured against the identical frozen campaign, and this subsection reports that
second measurement. The declarations, the scenario and the event script are unchanged; the
same 120 forecasts are recomputed by the modified model, so every difference reported below
belongs to the model and to nothing else.

\subsubsection{The Repair That Was Recommended Does Not Work}

Section~\ref{sec:res_discussion} proposed making the calibrated events write a lead-time
impact alongside their existing effects. Implemented and measured, that repair fails, and the
reason generalises beyond this system. A completion date estimated as a full production cycle
beginning now multiplies the lead time by the fraction of work remaining, so any quantity
routed into the lead time is multiplied by that fraction too. A shock therefore attenuates in
proportion to how close a milestone is to completion, and vanishes altogether on the
milestones nearest their deadline, which are precisely those on which a warning has value. On
NovaFab, the node carrying the scenario's accident, the four-week probability fell from
$0.846$ to $0.000$ on a milestone that was in fact missed.

Lost production time is additive, not proportional: a four-week freeze costs four weeks
whether the order is a tenth or nine tenths complete. The completion model was rewritten
accordingly, identically in the three independent estimators the system carries for the same
quantity, the analytic $u_{\text{time}}$ block, the Monte Carlo forecast rollout and the PERT
lead-time simulator:
\begin{equation}
    C \;=\; t \;+\; (1 - p)\,L \;+\; R,
    \label{eq:completion_repaired}
\end{equation}
where $p$ is the declared progress, $L$ the lead time and $R$ the time already lost. Five
families of capacity-halting event, machine failure, accident, strike weighted by the share
of workforce involved, material shortage weighted by criticality, and cyber incident, now
accumulate their effective duration into $R$. Loss of storage capacity deliberately does not,
since it removes space rather than production hours.

The mechanical effect is unambiguous. Across the whole of arm A, eight nodes and 41 indicator
snapshots each, the accumulated-delay field was written a non-zero value exactly zero times;
this is the strongest available form of the blindness claim of
Section~\ref{sec:res_mechanism}, since it does not depend on any scoring choice. After the
repair, NovaFab accumulates $154.7$ engine hours at its first shock and $371.2$ over the
campaign, carried in 27 of its 41 snapshots.

\subsubsection{Two Further Defects Exposed by the First}

With the time block responsive, a second defect became measurable. The milestone-miss
probability returned exactly $0$ or exactly $1$ in $85.0\%$ of the 120 forecasts, leaving 14
anywhere in between. That is a switch rather than a probability, and two declarants recorded
as much in free text before it was measured. The cause is that the rollout drew the lead time
at random while taking the declared progress as an exact value. Declared progress is the least
reliable input the system receives, and it belongs to precisely the class of quantity the
framework exists to doubt: the adequacy score is built on the premise that self-reported
figures run optimistic, and the forecast layer was then treating one of them as certain.
Progress is now drawn per replicate, with a standard deviation widened node by node in
proportion to that node's own measured hidden risk $H$, so the system's adequacy measurement
feeds its own forecast.

The third defect was in the data rather than the model, and it would have invalidated any
calibration performed before it was found. Indicator coverage on the campaign database was
$25.6\%$: 24 of the 42 indicators were empty on all eight nodes, the carbon and cost blocks
were inert everywhere, and only three of the six urgency blocks contributed. The lead time
itself was present on five nodes of eight, and the remaining three received a risk floor of
zero through the documented fallback for a missing lead time, which is an absence of data
presenting itself as a reassuring prediction. Completing the indicators from public figures
of the 2020 to 2022 shortage raised coverage to $49.1\%$ and made all six blocks active. One
model bug was undetectable until then: a per-node recovery capability, a 26-week aerospace
requalification time, was being read as production time already lost, pinning that node's
time block at $1.0$ from the first turn. An empty database conceals the errors of the model
that consumes it, which is a methodological point independent of this framework.

enter a probabilistic forecast as a certainty, not on the AUC.

horizon, so the forecast is still worse calibrated than a forecaster that announces the base
rate and stops; the repair moves the system from badly wrong to less wrong, not to useful.
The milestone probability remains binary in $71.7\%$ of cases against $85.0\%$ before, so the
over-confidence is reduced rather than removed. And the post-hoc calibration layer that
Section~\ref{sec:res_discussion} recommended was trained, measured, and deliberately not
connected: on the synthetic factory that produced its training data it improves the Brier
skill by $30.8\%$ and the AUC from $0.792$ to $0.850$, but applied to the campaign it improves
calibration at every horizon while dropping the four-week AUC from $0.721$ to $0.580$. Its
training label is an event striking the node at exactly $t+1$; the product displays a missed
milestone or a critical event cumulated over $(t, t+4]$. The layer cannot rank the cases
driven by the milestone channel because it has never seen one. This is the trap of
Section~\ref{sec:res_target_trap} encountered from the opposite direction: a calibration
fitted against the wrong target degrades a system that works, exactly as an evaluation
against the wrong target can condemn one. Connecting the layer requires the training label to
be rebuilt on the product's target, which requires the synthetic factory's snapshots to
record milestone truth, which they do not; that is the next concrete step rather than an
open research question.

\subsubsection{A Third Arm, and What It Does Not Show}

The repaired system was then exercised through a full nineteen-turn campaign in closed loop,
in which the same eight agents no longer only declared but also selected actions from a fixed
catalogue, applied to the chain by the mutation service and recorded in the intervention
journal: promote a backup arc, replan a milestone, expedite a shipment, raise capacity, revise
a declaration, or do nothing. One hundred and fifty-two declarations were recorded with no
unresolved consistency failure. The anti-contamination protocol of
Section~\ref{sec:methods_controls} was tightened for this arm: every message reaching an agent
is written by the tool into that agent's own sandbox, and a failed consistency check is
answered with the interface's own guidance rather than with a coordinator's instruction.

The headline figure flatters the framework and should not be used. Six of eleven milestones
were missed in the frozen scenario and none was left unfinished in the closed loop, which
reads as six saved. Against the original commitments it is not: five of eleven milestones
were met on their original date in the frozen run, and five of eleven in the closed loop. The
loop changed which milestone was met, not how many. One was genuinely saved, an electronics
series delivered on its original date that had slipped in the frozen truth. Four late
deliveries became replannings announced in advance rather than slips discovered at the
deadline, which has value to a customer without being a save. One milestone was replanned
that would have been met, which is the measurable cost of over-reaction. With eleven
milestones neither the benefit nor the cost is statistically established, and both are
reported as facts of one campaign rather than as results.

Two observations from that arm do carry. The arm B disarming reproduced exactly, eight
agents of eight at first exposure. And the causal chain the campaign showed to be broken now
closes: where arm A's foundry manager recorded that the tool was holding a low position
through a widely reported crisis and took no action, the repaired run moved the same node's
four-week probability from $0.088$ to $0.214$ at the shock turn and the agent replanned its
milestone. One reserve on internal validity must be stated: the model changed twice during
that campaign, at turns 15 and 16, so turns 16 to 18 are not comparable with what precedes
them. Every behavioural observation reported above is drawn from turns strictly earlier than
15.

\subsubsection{A Hypothesis That Became Measurable Again}

The criticality index degenerates under saturation, which forced H3 to be judged on the ten informative turns of nineteen and the other nine to be excluded. That exclusion rule is no longer necessary. The ranking now carries a log-survival key, $\ell(p) = -\ln(1-p)$ computed on an $\varepsilon$-regularised twin of the same propagation, which orders identically to $\Delta U_r$ outside saturation, a property-based invariant with a test to match, and keeps separating nodes inside it.

Re-measured on the repaired run, the two criteria decompose cleanly on the same data. The old criterion alone leaves 10 informative turns with NovaFab top-1 on 9 of them; adding the $\Delta \ell$ key leaves 17 informative turns of 19 with NovaFab top-1 on 16, a share of $94\%$ against a pre-registered threshold of $80\%$. Holding the criterion fixed instead and changing only the model, the frozen campaign gives $70\%$ and the repaired one $90\%$, so completing the indicators moves the figure as much as the new key does.

This is reported as exploratory and does not re-decide H3, for the same reason given for H4 in Section~\ref{sec:hypothesis_status}: a hypothesis re-run against a changed model no longer tests what the register asked, and two things changed at once here. Only the comparison of the two criteria on one run isolates a single cause. The defensible claim is the narrow one: the degeneracy that made nearly half the campaign unreadable is largely gone, and the quantity H3 tests now sits above its threshold on the repaired system.

\subsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:res_discussion}
%% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\paragraph{The circularity problem is the central methodological finding.}
Across the dry run, arm A and arm B, the same difficulty recurs in three different forms.
In the dry run, synthetic declared urgency was anchored on real urgency by construction, so
the gap the system exists to measure could not appear. In arm A, agents read their briefings
carefully and declared close to what the briefing described, so the gap again did not
appear, with $H$ peaking at $0.017$ where the hypothesis required $0.3$. In arm B, showing
the model's own output to the declarant closed the loop explicitly, so the declaration is
no longer independent of the model at all. A system that measures the divergence between a
human judgment and a computed state is only measuring anything when those two quantities
have independent sources, and preserving that independence is harder than it appears.

\paragraph{Ordering and level are separate problems needing separate fixes.}
The scores of Section~\ref{sec:res_armA_scored} do not support a single verdict on the
forecast layer. On this campaign it neither orders well nor announces well, but the
evidence for those two claims is of very different strength: discrimination rests on between
two and six positive observations and is therefore inconclusive, while the calibration
failure rests on 21 confident forecasts none of which materialised and is not plausibly a
sampling artefact. The remedies differ accordingly. Miscalibration of this shape is
routinely corrected by a post-hoc layer without touching the underlying
model~\autocite{niculescu2005predicting,guo2017calibration}. Poor discrimination is not, and
would require the model to change.

\paragraph{The specific defect is repairable and general, but not in the obvious way.}
The mechanism identified in Section~\ref{sec:res_mechanism} is not a scenario artefact. The
$u_{\text{time}}$ block computes milestone risk from a lead-time distribution against a
deadline, while the event engine writes to capacity, cost and failure probability. No path
connects a shock to any quantity on the time axis, so the block cannot respond to a
disruption, and any deployment of this framework would inherit that gap. The natural repair
is to make the calibrated events write a lead-time impact. Section~\ref{sec:res_repair}
reports that this was implemented, measured, and found to be wrong: a quantity routed into
the lead time is scaled by the fraction of work remaining and therefore vanishes on exactly
the milestones nearest their deadline. Lost time has to be added to the completion date, not
multiplied into it. The general form of the lesson is that a parameter describing a node's
capacity to recover and a state accumulating what it has already lost carry the same units
and are easy to confuse, and the confusion stays invisible while the database is
incomplete.

\paragraph{Communicating ignorance is a design requirement, not a nicety.}
The arm B result reframes what an advisory layer owes its reader. Faced with an exogenous
shock, a weather event, a fire, a geopolitical decision, the model has no basis for
prediction at all. Displaying a low probability in a confident format is then worse than
displaying nothing, because it converts an absence of information into an apparent
reassurance. Two mitigations follow directly and neither requires new science: make the
horizon salient, so that a low four-week probability cannot be read as a general statement of
safety, and state explicitly which classes of risk the model does not cover.

\paragraph{Threats to validity.}
Four limit what the above can support. The declarants are language models, so the external
validity of any claim about human declaration behaviour is unestablished; this is the
principal threat, and it is why arm B is reported as a measured effect on this population
rather than as a finding about operators. The campaign is a single scenario, so the outcome
base rate, the event mix and the milestone structure are all properties of one script. The
positive class is very small, which bounds every discrimination claim. Finally, arm B is
not blinded, since the agents are told they are being shown a predictive analysis; a design
in which some turns show a deliberately uninformative forecast would separate the effect of
the content from the effect of the display.

\paragraph{Against the pre-registered thresholds.}
Five of the six hypotheses are not confirmed and one, H5 on urgency contagion, is confirmed.
That is a largely negative result against the register, and it is reported as such. The
value of the campaign lies less in those verdicts than in the diagnosis they made possible:
the register asked whether the signal works, and the answer, with its mechanism, is that one
identifiable block prevents it from working and that showing the resulting forecast to the
declarant is actively counterproductive until that block is fixed.

\subsection{Opening: Grounding Real Urgency in Observed Capacity}
\label{sec:res_volume}
%% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsubsection{Motivation}

The framework above has a structural dependency that the campaign brought into focus. Every
KPI reaching the six blocks arrives either through a manual weekly review or through a
scripted event, so a node's score is never fresher than its last submitted review, and the
capacity block in particular depends on the same operator whose declaration the adequacy
score exists to audit. The independence that Section~\ref{sec:res_discussion} identifies as
the precondition for the whole measurement is therefore not structurally guaranteed.

A second system, developed alongside the main work, addresses one part of that dependency. It
estimates the physical capacity of a warehouse from public satellite imagery, which is an
observation no operator supplies and none can bias. This subsection reports it at the depth
that claim requires. It is an opening rather than a second contribution: the two systems have
not been connected, and no claim is made that they have.

\subsubsection{Method and Calibrated Constants}

The pipeline takes a coordinate, retrieves the building footprint from the French national
topographic database, retrieves 20~cm orthophotography and, where available, a LiDAR surface
model, and derives a storable volume and a transfer capacity. Its four constants are
calibrated rather than assumed, and each is reported with the sample behind it in
Table~\ref{tab:volume_constants}.

The storable volume follows as $0.80 \times \text{footprint} \times \text{height}$ and the

variation of $9.6\%$ on the dock pitch is the result that makes the second of these
generalisable: because the pitch is stable across sites, capacity depends only on how much
facade is equipped, and no per-site measurement of the pitch is needed.

One constraint is enforced throughout and is worth stating because it is unusual. The
regulatory database of classified installations is used only to calibrate the fill factor,
never to infer a result for a specific site. Many warehouses fall below the classification
thresholds and do not appear in it at all, so a system that inferred from it would silently
fail on exactly the smaller sites a supply chain twin most needs to see.

One of these four constants does not survive audit, and the discrepancy is reported here
rather than left in the code. The fill factor of $0.80$ is the value implemented in
production and attributed in the source to a 122-site calibration, but the detailed output of
that run was not retained. The corpus that is still on disk contains 68 matched sites, and
recomputing the same ratio over it gives a median of $0.97$ with an interquartile range of
$[0.80,\ 2.00]$, with only $47\%$ of sites falling inside the $[0.7,\ 1.4]$ band against the
$70\%$ the calibration script itself sets as its acceptance target
(Figure~\ref{fig:volume_calibration}). Two readings are possible and the available data does
not separate them: either the retained corpus is a biased remnant of the original sample, or
the production constant understates the true fill factor by roughly twenty per cent. Either
way the constant is not currently reproducible from the artifacts, which is why recollecting
the regulatory corpus cleanly and rederiving the factor is listed in
Section~\ref{sec:future_work} rather than presented as settled. The volumetric figures in
this section should be read with that uncertainty attached; the dock capacity results, which
depend on the pitch constant rather than on the fill factor, are unaffected.

\subsubsection{The Physical Limit and the Reframing}

The original objective was to count loading docks. That objective is unreachable, and
establishing why is the most transferable result of this work.

An empty dock door under a canopy produces no measurable signal from directly overhead.
Measured contrast at verified empty bays is $0.18\sigma$, $0.22\sigma$ and $0.01\sigma$
across a full sweep of assumed depths, while occupied bays on the same facades register at
$5.3\sigma$ to $6.4\sigma$. The measurement is therefore sensitive enough; the signal is
absent. Repeating the test on 8~cm very-high-resolution imagery leaves the doors equally
invisible, which shows the cause to be occlusion by the canopy roof rather than insufficient
resolution. Any method claiming to count empty doors one by one from a nadir view is
recovering an artefact.

Figure~\ref{fig:volume_resolution} shows the same equipped facade at both resolutions,
20~cm on the left and 8~cm on the right. The trailers, the apron and the dark canopy band
along the building edge are legible at 20~cm and sharper still at 8~cm, yet no individual
door resolves inside the canopy band at either scale. A gain of $2.5\times$ in ground sample
distance recovers no additional door. What improves with resolution is everything except the
quantity of interest.

That finding splits the problem in two, and the distinction should not be blurred:

\begin{itemize}
    \item counting each door individually is not observable at nadir, and no amount of
    resolution changes it;
    \item recognising that a facade is equipped for docking, and delimiting its extent, is
    observable, because the canopy, the apron and the bay rhythm remain visible whether or
    not a vehicle is present.
\end{itemize}

The second formulation is learnable, and it is sufficient, because capacity is a function of
equipped length rather than of individually identified doors. Figure~\ref{fig:volume_proof}
is the direct evidence. It shows three facades, of 135~m, 105~m and 15~m, on which the
automatic trailer detector found no vehicle at all. Individual doors are not resolvable on
any of them, yet the regular bay markings and the canopy edge remain plainly legible, which
is exactly the distinction the reframing turns on.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering

    \caption{Equipped facades on which no trailer was detected}
    \label{fig:volume_proof}
\end{figure}

One reservation is recorded honestly. The proportion of sites showing no usable dock
signature was measured on a subsample that required vehicles to be present to establish
ground truth, and is therefore biased toward sites with vehicles. It should be
re-measured on an independent draw.

A related finding constrains the zero-shot approach that preceded the learned model. Parked

answer.

A second methodological point concerns what is measured. Standard detection metrics score
shape overlap, but the operational quantity here is capacity, which depends only on the
length of the equipped zone. A polygon drawn too deep, or with imprecise edges, still yields
the correct dock count. The project therefore scores itself on the error in the derived dock
count rather than on mAP, and that business metric, not the detection metric, is the one
used to decide whether a model is good enough to deploy. On the reference run that metric
reported a global bias of $-35\%$, a median error of $62\%$ on images containing docks, and
$89\%$ of true negatives correctly left empty, which is a system that is directionally
useful and quantitatively not yet trustworthy.

\subsubsection{Annotation Integrity}

One episode in this work bears directly on research method and is reported for that reason.
An automated third-party annotation process produced 442 annotations and reported its own
output as verified and complete. The annotations were unusable: the process had placed a
dock zone along every edge of every building without examining the imagery at all, then
subdivided each zone at the nominal pitch, producing an implausible $88\%$ positive rate.

All of it was discarded and the dataset was re-annotated by hand. An audit of the manual
work found 11 genuine problems across 687 annotated zones, a defect rate of $1.6\%$. A
verification tool was then written and placed ahead of training, checking format, geometry
and consistency against the project's own measured thresholds, so that no external
annotation enters the pipeline unaudited. That tool caught a defect in its own first
implementation, where distance was measured to the largest building in a scene rather than
to all buildings, generating 26 false alarms on correct annotations of neighbouring
buildings.

The transferable point is that a confident self-assessment from an automated process is not
evidence, and that the cost of the verification step was small relative to the cost of
training on corrupted labels.

\subsubsection{What This Can and Cannot Feed}

The pipeline runs end to end on a geographic area, retrieving footprints, presenting each to
the trained model, assigning predicted zones to the correct building and writing capacity
figures (Figure~\ref{fig:volume_deployment}). One defect found during that deployment is
worth recording, because it illustrates the difference between a model error and a systems
error.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.82\linewidth]{Images/5.Volume/deployment_zone.png}
    \caption{End-to-end output on one deployment site}
    \label{fig:volume_deployment}
\end{figure} The national topographic database
fragments a single large warehouse into several polygons, so a 434~m dock apron was initially
attributed in full to a 5\,149~m\textsuperscript{2} fragment, yielding an implausible 221
docks per hectare against a corpus median of 7.9. The fix was geometric rather than
statistical: each predicted zone is now clipped so that a building receives only the portion
facing its own facade, and the quality control was changed from a density threshold, which
must be calibrated per site type, to the ratio of dock length to building perimeter, which is
dimensionless and cannot legitimately exceed one.

The honest boundary is as follows. The system supplies a storable volume, a pallet-position
estimate and, where a dock zone is detected, a transfer capacity and an occupancy ratio.
Those map onto the capacity block of Equation~\ref{eq:ur_local} and onto nothing else: it
says nothing about lead time, cost or emissions. Coverage is metropolitan France, bounded by
the public data services it draws on. The trained segmentation model is not yet wired into
the production capacity path, which still uses the zero-shot trailer method and its $36\%$
coverage. And the imagery is a snapshot, so the resulting figure is a structural capacity
rather than a live occupancy.

What it does change is the provenance of one input. A capacity figure derived from imagery
is not supplied by the operator whose declaration the adequacy score audits, which is
precisely the independence Section~\ref{sec:res_discussion} identifies as the precondition
for the measurement to mean anything. Figure~\ref{fig:volume_bridge} states where that input
would enter and, equally important, which blocks it would leave untouched.

\subsection{Synthesis Against the Research Questions}
\label{sec:res_synthesis}
%% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\paragraph{RQ1, multi-block real urgency.} A six-block noisy-OR aggregation with bidirectional
DAG propagation was specified, implemented and exercised on a nineteen-turn campaign across
eight nodes. The construction works as designed and is exactly decomposable, which is what
allowed its own defect to be found. The answer is qualified rather than clean: five of the
six blocks behaved as intended, and the sixth, the time block, was shown to be structurally
unable to respond to a disruption, because the event engine wrote to no quantity it reads.
That path was subsequently built and measured (Section~\ref{sec:res_repair}), which closes
the defect without settling the question: the aggregation is only as informative as its
inputs, and the campaign that produced the negative result ran with three of the six blocks
inert for want of data.

\paragraph{RQ2, cognitive adequacy formulation.} The asymmetric Prospect-Theory formulation
produces a bounded, interpretable score whose every term can be reconstructed, as
demonstrated on the Orbitalys node. Its answer splits by the granularity at which validity is
asked. At node level the score discriminates and does so early:
Section~\ref{sec:res_targeting} reports six consecutive turns on which the flagged set is a
subset of the eventually failing set, with no false alarm, ahead of any public sign of the
crisis. At the granularity of a probability attached to a node-week, predictive validity was
not demonstrated: at the pre-registered threshold the signal returns precision and recall of
zero, and the underlying forecast layer shows no discrimination above chance under the
production outcome definition while being substantially worse calibrated than a base-rate
forecaster. Section~\ref{sec:res_mechanism} attributes that second failure to one identified
and repairable cause rather than to the formulation, and Section~\ref{sec:res_repair} reports
the repair: the Brier deficit roughly halves at every horizon and the discrimination rises,
while the skill remains negative. The formulation is therefore usable today for triage and
not yet for probability, and the distance between the two is now measured rather than
argued.

\paragraph{RQ3, network prioritisation and criticality.} AHP and Fuzzy BWM elicitation,
PROMETHEE~II outranking and systematic shock simulation were combined into a working
ranking that placed the expected bottleneck at the top. Two documented behaviours bounded
it. The criticality index degenerated when many nodes saturated at once, which is common in
precisely the network-wide crisis where it would be most useful; that one has since been
addressed by a log-survival sort key which leaves the ranking unchanged outside saturation
and recovers 7 of the 9 saturated campaign turns, and the measurement is in
Section~\ref{sec:res_repair}. The second stands: the outranking inherited a perfect
correlation between two of its criteria on this run, which the tool flagged on its own.

\paragraph{RQ4, operational validation and the effect of showing the forecast.} The framework
was operationalised as a persistent digital twin and exercised through a pre-registered
weekly-cadence campaign with a control arm and a treatment arm on a frozen scenario. Five of
six hypotheses are not confirmed and H5 on urgency contagion is confirmed. On the second
half of the question the answer is unambiguous within its population: at first exposure, all
eight declarants were either reassured or confirmed by the displayed forecast, none revised
upward, and the scenario's critical event arrived two turns later.
