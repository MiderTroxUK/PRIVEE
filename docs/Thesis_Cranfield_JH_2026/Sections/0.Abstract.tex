In mutualised logistics networks the urgency an operator declares often diverges from the
urgency computable from the operational state. Over-declaration wastes shared capacity;
under-declaration hides rupture risk. No existing digital twin measures that divergence or
propagates it across a supplier network.

This thesis builds and tests one. Real urgency is aggregated at each node from six KPI blocks
through a weighted probabilistic OR and propagated upstream across a directed acyclic supply
graph; declared urgency is elicited through a consistency-gated pairwise questionnaire and
propagated downstream; their mismatch is scored on $[0,100]$ with an asymmetric
Prospect-Theory penalty. The system was evaluated through a pre-registered campaign replaying
the 2020 to 2022 semiconductor shortage over nineteen turns and eight nodes, with a blind
control arm and a forecast-assisted treatment arm on a frozen scenario.

The results separate two claims that are easily conflated. The gap carries information about
which supplier will fail: over the six turns preceding the public onset of the crisis, the
nodes it flags are, without a single false alarm, nodes that go on to miss a delivery
commitment, three of the four that eventually do, and the worst-scoring among them is flagged
eight turns before its miss while its own manager declares an urgency of $0.001$. The signal
then weakens for a structural reason rather than a defect, since declared urgency converges
on computed urgency once the crisis is visible and a measure defined as their difference
closes by construction. This bounds the instrument to early warning, which is where a warning
is worth most.

Converting that signal into a calibrated probability attached to a named week is a further
step, and it failed on the first attempt, diagnostically and precisely. The forecast layer
showed no discrimination above chance under the production outcome definition and was
severely miscalibrated, at a Brier skill between $-3.4$ and $-6.9$ against a base-rate
forecaster. Forecasts below $0.1$ were well calibrated, while twenty-one announcing a mean
probability of $0.948$ were followed by the predicted outcome exactly zero times. One block
explains it: the time block read a lead-time distribution that no shock ever reached. In the treatment arm, on the turn the forecast first became visible,
all eight declarants revised their urgency downward or held it and none revised upward, two
turns before the scenario's critical event. Both arms carry identical forecasts, so the effect
belongs to the display. The outcome definition itself moves the answer by more than any
modelling decision taken here: a naive definition improves the apparent skill fivefold while
leaving discrimination unchanged, and judging a milestone against the deadline first
committed to rather than the deadline it currently carries raises the four-week AUC from
$0.485$ to $0.654$.

The diagnosis was then acted on and re-measured on the identical frozen forecasts. The
obvious repair, having the events write a lead-time impact, proved wrong under measurement:
lost time must be added to the completion date rather than multiplied into it, or a shock
attenuates to nothing on the milestones nearest their deadline. After repair the Brier
deficit roughly halves at every horizon, from $-0.553$ to $-0.216$ at four weeks, and remains
negative. A closed-loop campaign on the repaired system, in which the same agents act on the
chain, meets five milestones of eleven on their original date, exactly as many as the frozen
run: it changes which milestone is met, not how many.

What bounds every figure above is the sample rather than the construction: eight nodes of
which four are positive, eleven observable milestones, one scripted scenario, and
language-model declarants. The instrument and its diagnostic method transfer to any supply
chain; the rates measured here do not yet transfer to anything, and widening that base is the
work this thesis hands on.
